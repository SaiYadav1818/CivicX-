## 2. Objective

Build an authority-mapping system that identifies the appropriate government authority and department responsible for a reported civic issue.

Examples:
- Garbage accumulation → sanitation department.
- Road pothole → roads engineering department.
- Water leakage → water supply department.
- Drainage blockage → drainage or sewerage department.
- Broken streetlight → electrical/streetlight department.

These are examples only. Actual jurisdiction and responsibility must be verified.

## 4. Mapping engine

Implement:

ComplaintAuthorityMappingService

Inputs:
- Complaint category.
- GPS coordinates.
- Address.
- Ward ID.
- Zone ID.

Output:
- Suggested authority.
- Suggested department.
- Ward and zone.
- Mapping confidence or rule explanation.
- Whether manual verification is required.

Use deterministic mapping rules first.

Do not allow AI to independently determine legal jurisdiction.

GPS coordinates alone must not be treated as proof of municipal jurisdiction.

Support:
- Missing GPS.
- Boundary locations.
- Conflicting ward and address data.
- Unknown localities.
- Multiple potentially responsible departments.

When uncertain, mark the mapping as NEEDS_REVIEW.

## 6. Mapping workflow

1. Citizen submits a complaint.
2. CivicX identifies the category and location.
3. Mapping engine finds possible authority.
4. System checks whether the mapping is verified.
5. Uncertain cases go to admin review.
6. Admin confirms or corrects the mapping.
7. Mapping result is stored with its history.

Do not automatically submit complaints to external government portals in this phase.
