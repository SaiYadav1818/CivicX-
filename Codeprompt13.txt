# PROMPT 13 — CivicX Smart Product Finder: Ask Nearby Shops

You are a senior full-stack engineer working on my existing CivicX project.

Implement a new independent feature called **CivicX Ask Nearby Shops**.

First inspect the actual repository, frontend, backend, database schema, authentication, shop registration, product catalogue, locality system, notification infrastructure and existing CivicX Local Shops module.

Do not guess or rewrite existing functionality.

## 1. Product Vision

The purpose of this feature is to help customers find products when they do not know which nearby shop sells them.

Example:

A customer needs a particular mobile charger but does not know which local shop has it.

Instead of visiting multiple shops, the customer opens CivicX, uploads a photo of the charger or types its name, and sends one request to nearby relevant shop owners.

Nearby shops receive the request and can respond:

- YES, AVAILABLE.
- AVAILABLE IN ANOTHER BRAND.
- OUT OF STOCK.
- CAN ARRANGE IT.
- NOT AVAILABLE.

The customer can compare responses, see shop details and visit the shop.

The feature should connect customers with existing approved CivicX Local Shops.

Do not create duplicate shop registration or authentication systems.

## 2. Customer Request Creation

Create an Ask Nearby Shops section.

Customers should be able to:

1. Open CivicX.
2. Select Ask Nearby Shops.
3. Upload a product photo OR enter the product name.
4. Add a description.
5. Select a product category.
6. Enter brand or model, if known.
7. Enter quantity required.
8. Select locality.
9. Select search radius.
10. Submit the request.

Example:

Product: Mobile Charger

Photo: Uploaded charger image.

Brand: Samsung

Description: "I need a Samsung 25W Type-C charger."

Quantity: 1

Locality: Street 9

Search radius: 1 km or 3 km.

The customer must be able to submit a request using text alone, a photo alone, or both.

Photo upload must be optional.

## 3. Product Categories

Use existing shop categories and product categories wherever possible.

Support:
- Groceries
- Mobile phones and accessories
- Electronics
- Hardware
- Electrical items
- Plumbing materials
- Building materials
- Clothing
- Footwear

- Medicines and pharmacy products, subject to applicable regulations
- Household products
- Kitchen items
- Other products

Allow admins to manage categories.

Do not create duplicate category systems if the existing Local Shops module already provides them.

## 4. Nearby Shop Matching

When a customer submits a request, identify relevant approved shops.

Match using:

- Customer locality.
- Search radius.
- Shop service area.
- Shop category.
- Product category.
- Shop approval status.
- Shop active status.

Example:

A customer requests a Samsung charger in Street 9.

Eligible nearby shops:

- Sai Mobile Store — 500 metres.
- Sri Electronics — 1 km.
- Kumar Mobile Accessories — 2 km.

Send the request only to relevant shops.

Do not send a mobile charger request to unrelated grocery stores.

Do not send requests to suspended or unapproved shops.

Do not send new requests to shops that have disabled request notifications.

Avoid duplicate delivery of the same request to the same shop.

## 5. Shop Owner Notifications

Shop owners should receive notifications when a matching customer request is available.

Example:

"New customer request near Street 9."

Product: Samsung 25W Type-C Charger.

Quantity: 1.

The shop owner can open the request and respond.

Allow shop owners to control whether they want to receive nearby product requests.

Do not reveal the customer's full home address or personal contact details to shops.

Show approximate locality only.

## 6. Shop Owner Responses

Provide simple response buttons.

### Option A — Available

Shop owner selects YES, AVAILABLE.

They must enter:

- Product brand.
- Product model, if applicable.
- Available quantity.
- Price.
- Optional product photo.
- Stock confirmation time.

### Option B — Alternative Available

Shop owner selects AVAILABLE IN ANOTHER BRAND.

They provide:

- Available brand.
- Model.
- Price.
- Description.
- Optional photo.

### Option C — Out of Stock

Shop owner selects OUT OF STOCK.

### Option D — Can Arrange

Shop owner selects CAN ARRANGE.

They provide an estimated time when the product can be arranged.

### Option E — Not Available

Shop owner selects NOT AVAILABLE.

All responses should be associated with the original customer request.

## 7. Customer Response Comparison

This is the most important customer screen.

When shops respond, show a list of responses.

Example:

Customer request: Samsung 25W Type-C Charger.

Responses:

Shop 1:

- 500 metres away.
- Available.

- ₹420.
- Confirmed 10 minutes ago.

Shop 3:
- Kumar Mobile Accessories.
- 2 km away.
- Alternative available.
- Other brand.
- ₹350.

The customer should be able to:

- Compare prices.
- Compare brands.
- View product photos.
- See shop distance.
- See response time.
- View shop opening status.
- Open the shop profile.
- Call the shop.
- Get directions to the shop.
- Mark a shop as selected.

These prices and shop names are illustrative examples only.

Never display fake availability or fake shop responses.

## 8. Stock Confirmation and Expiry

A shop owner clicking YES must not mean that stock is guaranteed indefinitely.

Implement:

- Response timestamp.
- Stock confirmation timestamp.
- Configurable response validity.
- Expired response status.
- Shop owner ability to withdraw an offer.
- Customer visibility into how recently stock was confirmed.

Example:

"Available — confirmed 5 minutes ago."

If the response is old:

"Availability not recently confirmed. Contact the shop before visiting."

When a response expires, do not show it as currently confirmed.

Do not automatically reserve stock just because a shop responded YES.

Optionally support a future feature where shops can reserve an item for a customer.

## 9. Customer and Shop Contact

Customers should be able to:

- Call a responding shop.
- Open its profile.
- Use supported messaging/contact options.
- View directions.
- Mark the request as resolved.

Do not publicly expose phone numbers in search responses.

Only reveal contact information through the approved contact flow and according to shop-owner consent.

## 10. Request Lifecycle

Suggested request statuses:

- DRAFT
- OPEN
- RESPONSES_RECEIVED
- CUSTOMER_SELECTED_SHOP
- RESOLVED
- EXPIRED
- CANCELLED

Suggested shop response statuses:

- AVAILABLE
- ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE
- OUT_OF_STOCK
- CAN_ARRANGE
- NOT_AVAILABLE
- WITHDRAWN
- EXPIRED

Maintain request and response history.

Allow the customer to close or cancel a request.

Prevent shops from responding to expired or cancelled requests.

## 11. Optional AI Product Recognition

Make the feature future-ready for AI.

Possible future flow:

1. Customer uploads a product image.
2. AI suggests the likely product name and category.
3. Customer reviews and edits the suggestion.
4. Customer confirms the request.
5. CivicX sends the request to relevant shops.

Important:
- Do not claim AI has identified a product with certainty.
- Do not invent brand, model or specifications.
- Always allow manual text entry.
- Do not block request creation if AI recognition is unavailable.

Do not introduce an expensive AI service in the initial implementation unless the existing project already has a suitable integration.
